Frequently Asked Questions

01

Does having two mediators mean higher costs?

On the contrary. Because the process is more focused and efficient, and our fees are competitive, the overall cost for mediation is lower.

02

My wife and I have decided to separate. Do we each need to hire a lawyer?

There is no such requirement. Doing so may lead to unnecessary disputes that harm you and your children. It is possible, and preferable, to reach a fair, mutually acceptable divorce agreement without unnecessary expenses or battles. Family courts also encourage couples involved in divorce disputes to consider mediation, with a professional, impartial mediators guiding the dialogue.

03

I told my husband that I currently agree to try reconciliation. What if I still want a divorce?

You can pursue a marital reconciliation agreement with divorce as an alternative. This agreement sets out both the terms for rebuilding the relationship and living together and the terms for divorce if reconciliation ultimately fails, providing clarity and a safety net for either outcome.

04

I want a divorce. How can I avoid harming the children?

The way to protect children emotionally is to keep them out of the conflict and maintain a united parental front. Our mediation process helps de-escalate tensions, end the battles, and build a stable, healthy co-parenting partnership so your children can continue growing up feeling secure.

05

How long does divorce mediation take?

On average, about five weekly meetings are required. The duration depends on the agreement’s complexity, the number and scope of unresolved issues, and the parties’ willingness to cooperate.

06

Why is mediation with both a male and female mediator preferable?

A male–female mediation team offers greater personal reassurance to both parties, creates balance, and provides a broader range of professional knowledge and solutions.

07

How can we communicate despite the difficulty and pain?

Divorce is emotionally very difficult. It is a challenging period, and our extensive experience will help you navigate it with as little pain as possible.

08

What should a good, precise divorce agreement include?

A comprehensive, professionally drafted divorce agreement addresses every aspect of separation in detail: division of property and savings, housing and mortgage arrangements, a clear child-support framework, and a detailed plan for parental responsibility and parenting time appropriate to the children’s ages. Clear, precise wording helps prevent future misunderstandings.

09

Can a divorce agreement be changed after it is signed?

Once approved by the court, the agreement has the force of a judgment, and changes require court approval. This is why precision and detail matter. We prepare a thorough, carefully drafted agreement that addresses the issues and conditions unambiguously and reduces the need to revisit them.

10

We have decided to pursue divorce mediation. How should we handle this with the children?

During mediation, we also guide you in explaining and managing the separation with your children. The solutions we develop, the way you present the situation, and a united parental approach help provide the clarity and security your children need.

11

How is property divided in a divorce agreement?

Dividing assets, real estate, or family businesses requires transparent financial mapping, accurate valuations, and an analysis of pension and other employment-related entitlements. The aim is to protect both parties’ financial interests through creative solutions that avoid future disputes and unnecessary erosion of assets.

12

Where is a divorce agreement submitted, and how is it approved?

After the agreement is reached in mediation, we draft it with a high degree of legal precision and submit it for approval to the Family Court or Rabbinical Court. We accompany you until a short, formal hearing at which the judge or rabbinical judge gives the agreement the force of a binding judgment.

13

What happens if the mediation fails?

The mediation is confidential: statements made in the mediation room remain confidential and are generally inadmissible in legal proceedings. If irreconcilable differences arise, you may turn to the courts, but you will do so having reduced tensions, clarified the disputes, and potentially saved time and unnecessary expense.

14

When is the best time to begin mediation?

If you are considering separation or have decided to separate, it is advisable to pursue mediation to manage the divorce by mutual agreement and save considerable time and money compared with litigation. Mediation is also possible at any stage, even while court proceedings are underway. It helps you retain control, avoid unnecessary battles, and develop creative solutions reflecting your lives, wishes, and expectations.

15

How do we find a mediator who is truly right for us?

A good mediator combines empathy, emotional sensitivity, and compassion with strategic skills, logical thinking, and complete professionalism. It is also desirable for the mediation practice to be readily available for questions and follow-up arrangements outside meetings and to conduct sessions in a way that strengthens the couple’s sense of security and certainty.
