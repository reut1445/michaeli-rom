Special Expenses

In this article

Examples of Special Expenses

In addition to child support, if agreed, which is intended for regular expenses primarily associated with housing, parents also divide special expenses relating to the children.

The agreement specifies which expenses are included in child support and which count as special expenses. Because special expenses are shared on an ongoing basis, they must be defined precisely. The agreement states the percentage each parent will pay, generally based on the parents’ net income.

The agreement should also specify how reimbursements will be handled. Examples include reimbursement within a specified number of days, monthly reconciliation, or a dedicated account for children’s expenses, which can be convenient when there are many such costs.

Examples of Special Expenses

The following may be listed as special expenses if they are not already included in child support:

  • Clothing, including shoes
  • Exceptional medical treatments such as orthodontics, cosmetic procedures and psychological treatment
  • Extracurricular activities and summer camps
  • Private school tuition
  • Health insurance
  • Daycare, kindergarten and after-school care
  • School fees
  • Gifts
  • School supplies
  • Public transportation
  • Private tutoring
  • Psychometric exam preparation courses
  • Youth movements
  • Matriculation exam preparation
  • Remedial instruction
  • Study fees
  • Specialized academic tracks
  • Parent committee fees
  • Class committee fees
  • Annual school trips
  • School-organized trips abroad and delegations
  • Gym membership
  • Driving lessons
  • Driving theory and practical tests
  • Purchase of a vehicle for a child
  • Dental care, including orthodontics
  • Eye care, including glasses and contact lenses
  • Purchase and periodic upgrading of a mobile phone
  • Personal computer and/or iPad if needed for studies, including periodic upgrades
  • Cosmetic treatments
  • Laser treatments
  • Speech therapy
  • Psychological and psychiatric care and occupational therapy
  • Undergraduate and graduate studies in Israel or abroad
  • Bar or Bat Mitzvah

It is likely that the detailed expense list will not be needed if both parents understand that any child-related expense not covered by child support is an exceptional expense. Nevertheless, when there is uncertainty, the more detailed the list, the better it can prevent disputes.
