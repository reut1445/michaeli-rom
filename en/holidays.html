Dividing Holidays, Events, Vacations and Handover Times

In this article

Dividing HolidaysSchool VacationsHandover TimesFamily EventsIn Summary

Many subjects are discussed during mediation. One important area that often receives too little attention is the division of holidays and the times at which children move between homes. Poorly defined arrangements can become a source of unnecessary disputes later.

Dividing Holidays

At first glance, dividing holidays seems simple: split them equally. But different interpretations can cause considerable disagreement and tension between parents. Your agreement will include a holiday allocation table. Consider the following questions:

  • Are ordinary weekdays between holidays included in the holiday allocation?
  • Which holidays should be listed? For example, is Hanukkah treated as a holiday?
  • Are holidays alternated in a balanced way, such as Yom Kippur with one parent and the first half of Sukkot with the other?
  • Should Rosh Hashanah and Shavuot be split between the parents or kept intact?

School Vacations

In addition to holiday periods, there are other school breaks, such as the Passover break that begins about a week before Passover and the summer vacation.

During the pre-Passover break, the usual parenting-time schedule is commonly followed. During summer, you can make other arrangements, for example:

  • Continue the regular parenting-time schedule, with vacations as agreed by the parents.
  • Alternate weeks.
  • Follow another mutually agreed arrangement, reverting to the regular schedule if no agreement is reached.

Handover Times

Handover times can cause considerable tension and deserve careful attention. On school days, the handover generally occurs when school ends. But what happens when school is canceled or the child does not attend? What time applies at the beginning and end of a holiday? What time applies during school breaks? Children, especially teenagers, often sleep late on vacation mornings, so their needs should be considered.

Family Events

It is advisable to agree in advance what happens when an event involving one parent’s family falls during the other parent’s parenting time.

In Summary

Holiday allocations, vacations, and handover times are important. Careful attention to details prevents future tensions and makes life easier for your children.
